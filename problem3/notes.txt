The key difference is here:

- **7 artifacts:** the solver spends `0.778 → 31.079 s` proving layer 11 unsatisfiable.
- **8 artifacts:** it spends only `0.571 → 6.003 s` on the equivalent step.

Adding `artifact_8` increases the encoding by about 8%, but it also changes the SAT formula’s structure. Glucose’s branching decisions and learned clauses then happen to make the second formula much easier to prove. SAT solving is highly non-linear: a slightly larger formula can be substantially easier than a smaller one.

Other observations:

- Both runs reach the solution at **layer 12**.
- The number of instantiated positions, actions, and reductions is identical.
- The second run’s final plan is shorter: 80 steps versus 84.
- The planner is not simply executing one operation per artifact; it searches over many possible orderings, robot assignments, movements, and temporary actions.

Therefore, the 34 versus 15 seconds is expected solver variance caused by the altered constraint structure—not evidence that processing eight artifacts is intrinsically faster. Cold-start effects or CPU contention may contribute slightly, but the logs clearly show the SAT search as the main cause.
